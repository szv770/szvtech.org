How it works

Typing

Turn on Hebrew keys and tap the text box: a Hebrew keyboard slides up and the phone’s own keyboard stays away. Hold a letter for its vowels. The Nikud tab has every point, the Marks tab has trope, geresh, gershayim, maqaf and digits. Final letters fix themselves when you press space (turn that off below). Turn Hebrew keys off to use the phone’s own keyboard.

English → Hebrew rules

First a dictionary of common words ( spellings: Lubavitch, Moshiach, Chassidus, Shabbos, Hashem…). Otherwise sounds, in order: sh=ש ch=ח tz=צ kh=כ th=ת ph=פ zh=ז׳ j=ג׳, t=ת but t’=ט, k=כ, q=ק, a’ o’ e’=ע, s=ס, v=ו at the start of a word and ב inside it, w=ו. Vowels: a and e are left out inside a word (and become א at the start, ה at the end), o and u=ו, i=י, ai ei ay ey=י. Doubled letters count once. Final letters are fixed for you. It is a phonetic guess: check the result.

Hebrew → English rules

Ashkenazi / Chabad flavour: kamatz and cholam = o, patach = a, tzeirei = ei, segol = e, chirik = i, כ and ח = ch, צ = tz, ש = sh, ת without a dagesh = s (Shabbos), with one = t. With nikud the result is exact. Without nikud it uses the same dictionary, then guesses vowels (an a between consonants).

Rashi script

Rashi is a font, not different letters: the text underneath is normal Hebrew, so copying gives normal Hebrew. The page uses Noto Rashi Hebrew (a free Google font). “Print” is a book typeface and “Modern” a clean sans, to compare. Save as picture draws the same font into a PNG.

Privacy

Everything happens on your phone. Your draft and recent texts are saved only in this browser.
